1. Input-matched comparison on Lyft L5 (zero-shot)

1.1 Comparison with matched inputs

All methods receive the same real initial frame; 15.4 s generations (185 frames at 12 Hz).

Frame rate. Lyft L5 provides camera frames and 3D box annotations at 5 Hz. For all Lyft L5 videos on this page, we interpolate each instance's 3D box trajectory between its annotated keyframes to obtain smooth 12 Hz conditions, and every method generates 12 Hz video from them. The real video is shown at its native 5 Hz, with each frame held to match 12 Hz playback.

1.2 Quality over time with full-chunk fine-tuning

MemOIR fine-tuned on 25-frame clips and on full 93-frame chunks, with matched frame counts.

2. Consistency experiments

2.1 Forward–reverse revisit consistency on Lyft L5 (zero-shot)

The first chunk drives forward; the second replays the ego and instance trajectories in reverse, so every pedestrian and building is revisited.

2.2 Pedestrian identity after re-entering the frame across the chunk boundary (WalkingTours, Wan2.1-VACE)

The chunk boundary is placed while the target person is out of view; markers on the timeline show the chunk boundary and the return.

2.3 Face identity after a turn-away across the chunk boundary (CelebV-HQ, Wan2.1-VACE)

The chunk boundary is placed mid-turn, when only the profile or the back of the head is visible.

3. Robustness experiments

3.1 Noisy box conditioning (nuScenes)

One target vehicle per scene is perturbed in the conditioning canvas by location jitter (σ = 0.3 m per axis) or box dropout (30% of frames).

3.2 Identity switch within a chunk (nuScenes)

A vehicle briefly leaves the frame and returns with a different instance ID inside the same chunk.
